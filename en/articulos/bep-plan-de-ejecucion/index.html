      Information requirements

      From strategy to data.

      Requirements cascade down: from what the organisation needs to know about its assets to what each appointment must deliver.

      Four acronyms, one order
      The E in EIR Exchange formerly “Employer's”: now one per appointment
      The parties

      Who asks and who answers.

      The appointing party appoints a lead appointed party, which coordinates its appointed parties. Together they form the delivery team.

      Project team
      BIM execution plan

      The BEP answers the EIR.

      The EIR says what information the client wants. The BEP explains how the team will produce it, with what resources and under which standards.

      Delivery plans

      Each team, its own plan.

      Each task team writes its TIDP: which information containers it delivers and when. The lead appointed party combines them into a single MIDP.

      Information delivery milestones

      Information arrives when a decision is due.

      Milestones are set where the client needs data to make a decision, not when the team finishes modelling.

      Common data environment

      Four states per container.

      Every file moves from work in progress to shared, published and archived. Each step has a check and someone who signs it off.

      States and transitions
      Maintenance

      Updated, not filed away.

      The BEP is offered, confirmed at appointment and revised whenever something that affects the information changes: a stage, a team, a program.

      How to recognise one

      Specific, named and checkable.

      A BEP copied from another project shows: it speaks in generalities. A useful one answers each requirement with a person, a date and a way to check it.

      Filler BEP
      • “BIM software will be used”
      • “The team will coordinate the models”
      • “The standard will be followed”
      Useful BEP
      • Program, version and format per team
      • Weekly federation, named person in charge
      • IFC4 validated against the EIR’s IDS
      ISO 19650

      Six parts, one language.

      Concepts, delivery phase, operational phase, exchange, security, and health and safety. In Spain they are published as UNE-EN ISO 19650.

      ISO 19650-2

      Eight activities, two BEPs.

      Part 2 organises each appointment into eight activities. The BEP appears in the tender response and is confirmed at appointment.

      Where it comes from

      Born British, made ISO.

      The BEP and the EIR come from UK standards and moved into ISO in 2018. The revision under way could change their names.

      Timeline
      UK National Annex

      A name that explains itself.

      The UK annex proposes field-based names and a status code for every file. It is the model many CDEs and tender documents copy.

      Seven fields separated by hyphens

      Approval workflow

      From S0 to A1, signed off.

      Delivery formats

      Open formats, in the contract.

      The EIR sets the format for each deliverable and the BEP says which program and settings produce it. Spain’s Plan BIM requires IFC and BCF.

      IDS · buildingSMART

      The machine-readable EIR.

      With IDS, the part of the EIR that asks for data is written in XML that any checker reads the same way. The BEP says who runs it and when.

      In the EIR “All doors shall carry their fire rating.”
      In the IDS IfcDoor → Pset_DoorCommon
      FireRating required
