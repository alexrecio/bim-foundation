      B · How we measure the Earth

      A model for an irregular Earth.

      The Earth is neither a sphere nor a regular shape, and you cannot calculate on it as it is. The ellipsoid is a mathematical model that approximates its real shape: coordinates are calculated on it.

      Three surfaces, exaggerated
      Equatorial radius · a6 378kmGRS80 · 6 378 137 m
      Polar radius · b6 357kmflattened by about 21 km
      Geoid versus ellipsoid−106 to +86msea level, across the planet; relief adds kilometres
      Datum

      The datum ties the shape to the Earth.

      The geodetic datum anchors the ellipsoid to the Earth with a centre, axes and a date, and fixes position. Height is fixed separately: the vertical datum starts from mean sea level, measured at one or more tide gauges.

      Two datums: one for position, one for height
      Your country · SpainETRS89height: Alicante
      Asia and Oceania · AustraliaGDA2020height: AHD
      Americas · PeruSIRGASheight: La Punta (Callao)
      Geodetic networks

      The network puts the datum on the ground.

      GNSS stations and control points fix position; a tide gauge and levelling benchmarks, height.

      Projection

      Projecting means moving to a Euclidean plane.

      On the ellipsoid, neither a ruler nor Pythagoras works. The projection is an abstraction: a plane where you design with everyday geometry, in exchange for a small distortion.

      From the ellipsoid to the E · N plane
      On the plane d = √(ΔE² + ΔN²) distances, straight lines and drawing angles

      Floating point

      Far from the origin, everything jitters.

      With large coordinates, every program loses precision. That is why you do not model directly in UTM.

      The engine's usable zone
      UTM Y in Spain 4 400 km from the projection origin
      A UTM point in Spain E ≈ 400 000
      N ≈ 4 400 000

      Why it happens

      Integers eat the decimals.

      A floating-point number has a limited number of digits. With millions of metres, few are left for millimetres.

      Single precision: about 7 useful digits
      Smallest step at 4 474 km 0.5m single precision
      Smallest step at 310 m 0.03mm single precision
      The cost

      More digits, more bytes.

      Double precision doubles the size of each coordinate: it shows in a point cloud, not in a model.

      Single · float32 4bytes
      ≈ 7 digits
      Double · float64 8bytes
      ≈ 15–16 digits
      Point cloud of one billion points (X, Y, Z only)
      f6424 GB · lossless
      f3212 GB · loses 0.5 m
      LAS12 GB · to the millimetre

      Quality control

      A model can look right and be a hundred metres off. Before federating: where it is, which way it faces, at what elevation and in which system.
